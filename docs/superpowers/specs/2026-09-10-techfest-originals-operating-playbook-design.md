# Techfest 2026 bits&bytes Originals operating playbook design

## Purpose

Create one internal operating playbook for the bits&bytes™ team delivering twelve city zonal hackathons and a national final with Techfest at IIT Bombay. A new contributor should understand the programme in about twenty minutes and use the same document during weekly execution.

The playbook explains the partnership in plain English, records the current campaign plan, defines the sponsorship and outreach workflow, states the limits of contributor authority, and keeps unresolved decisions visible. It does not replace the signed partnership agreement or assign work to named team members.

## Source hierarchy

1. Applicable law and the Foundation's statutory documents.
2. The executed Partnership Agreement for Co Organisation with Techfest IIT Bombay, including Schedule 1 and Addenda A and B.
3. Written joint decisions made by authorised representatives of both parties.
4. Board approved Foundation policies and financial controls.
5. This playbook and its recorded revisions.
6. Team messages, call notes, and verbal instructions.

Where the playbook and a higher source conflict, the higher source controls and the playbook must be corrected.

## Programme facts

- Twelve city zonal hackathons will qualify participants for one national final.
- The final is planned for 17 and 18 December 2026 at IIT Bombay.
- Registrations are planned to open on 15 September 2026.
- Zonals are planned to begin in mid October and finish by 30 November 2026.
- The gross sponsorship target for the bits&bytes™ campaign is ₹2 crore.
- At least 80 percent of verified participants across the entire twelve city campaign should be high school students. This is not a city by city quota.
- The outreach network should grow to 50 to 100 active contributors.

## Event formats

The campaign may use three zonal formats. The format for each city remains an open decision until venue, budget, programme, and safeguarding readiness are confirmed.

| Format | Duration | Intended use |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| bits&bytes™ Originals Mini | 8 to 12 hours | A compact city event when an overnight programme is unnecessary or not supportable |
| bits&bytes™ Originals | 24 hours | The standard hackathon format, subject to overnight safeguarding readiness |
| bits&bytes™ Originals Extended | 36 to 48 hours | A deeper build format used only when venue, staffing, budget, and minor safeguarding controls support it |

## Campaign operating flow

The opening visual maps five phases: registrations open on 15 September; cities become operational during September and October; twelve zonals run from mid October through 30 November; finalists prepare from 1 through 16 December; and the national final runs at IIT Bombay on 17 and 18 December.

Fundraising runs through every phase. The standard sponsorship loop is research, approved outreach, proposal and close, cash received, sponsor delivery, reporting, and renewal. Only eligible cleared cash advances the ₹2 crore counter.

## Partnership summary

The playbook will describe Techfest as Lead Organiser and GOBITSNBYTES FOUNDATION as Co Organiser for the 30th edition. Techfest leads IIT Bombay campus access, venue infrastructure, security, networking, power, audiovisual logistics, institutional coordination, and broad corporate festival sponsorship. bits&bytes™ leads youth and builder outreach, city execution, technical tracks, problem statements, workshops, judging design, mentors, and developer focused sponsors.

Major budget changes, venue or date changes, material sponsor decisions, and significant public announcements require written agreement between the parties. Local Forks, volunteers, minors, and youth executives cannot bind the Foundation merely because they work on the campaign.

## Sponsorship model

The ₹2 crore figure is a gross cash sponsorship target for the bits&bytes™ campaign. It is not a promise of net surplus. It excludes GST, refunds, unpaid commitments, and in kind credits when progress is reported.

The user intends the agreement's 60 to 40 split to apply to eligible commercial activations at Techfest provided venues, including stalls, with 60 percent to the Foundation and 40 percent to Techfest. The executed agreement uses broader language for zonal sponsorship and on ground advertising revenue. The playbook must present the narrower treatment as an interpretation requiring written confirmation before a sponsor receives a commercial promise based on it.

All receipts must enter an official Foundation or Techfest account permitted by the agreement. Contributors may not receive event money through personal accounts, personal UPI IDs, wallets, or cash.

## Outreach and commission rules

The playbook will define one shared pipeline, one accountable owner per sponsor record, controlled access to contact data, approved sponsor inventory, written handoffs, and a weekly review. It will include a concise daily rhythm for research, outreach, follow up, proposal support, and record updates without assigning named staff.

Any approved contributor who sources a sponsor may earn a commission. The rate is decided case by case in writing, ranges from 1 percent to 3 percent, and can never exceed 3 percent. Commission is calculated only on eligible sponsorship cash that clears in the Foundation's official account. GST, refunds, unpaid commitments, and in kind support are excluded.

Eligibility to earn commission does not confer authority. Contributors cannot sign contracts, change approved packages, make unapproved sponsor promises, collect funds, or bind the Foundation. A minor contributor requires guardian documentation and finance approval before payout and remains outside all contracting and collection authority.

## Participants and safeguarding

The campaign wide high school share will use verified participation rather than raw signups. The dashboard will show total verified participants, verified high school participants, and the resulting percentage across completed zonals.

Every activity involving minors must follow the Foundation's safeguarding rules and the partnership agreement. The playbook will cover verifiable guardian consent for minor data processing, separate media consent, dual adult supervision, no isolated one to one sessions, incident escalation, and strict limits on participant data shared with sponsors.

Any 24 hour or Extended event involving minors must pass an overnight readiness check before public confirmation. The check includes controlled venue access, physical perimeter security, gender separated resting areas, designated chaperones, vetted adult supervision, emergency contacts, medical and incident procedures, guardian consent, and safe arrival and departure plans.

## City rollout records

The playbook will include twelve numbered blank city records rather than a speculative city list. Each record will capture selection status, proposed date, proposed format, host institution, venue status, participant capacity, local operating team, adult safeguarding coverage, budget readiness, sponsor status, and the next decision required.

## Programme controls

The weekly dashboard will track registrations, verified participants, campaign wide high school share, city readiness, sponsor pipeline value, eligible cash received, outreach contributors, content shipped, open safeguarding issues, overdue sponsor deliverables, decisions due, and the distance remaining to ₹2 crore.

The playbook will include a decision register and version history. Each open decision will state the decision required, responsible operating lane, deadline, current status, and the written record that closes it. Intentional open decisions include the twelve cities, format per city, exact zonal dates, city capacities, qualification counts, finalist travel, prizes, sponsor inventory, and detailed budgets.

## Visual direction

Use the approved campaign editorial direction. The cover uses the bits&bytes™ burgundy `#97192c`, orange `#fc920d`, black, and white with a strong sans serif title and a small serif description. Body pages are quiet, spacious, and optimised for lookup. Headings remain black. Burgundy and orange appear in restrained accents, table headers, status markers, and the operating map.

Use a professional sans serif for headings and a readable serif or neutral sans serif for body text, depending on renderer reliability. Tables use visible light gray borders, deliberate column widths, adequate padding, repeated header rows, and alternating pale fills when helpful. The document avoids decorative callout boxes, excessive border treatments, and dense poster like pages.
