# ⚡ Fork Onboarding Guide

Welcome to bits&bytes™. If you're reading this, you've either just been recognised as a Fork Lead, you're in the process of getting there, or you're a Local Maintainer joining an existing Fork. Either way — this is your starting point.

## Step 1 — Understand what a Fork actually is

A Fork is an official operational unit of bits&bytes™. It's the local face of the network in your city, institution, or community. You get to build local culture, run events, recruit contributors, and create a real builder community.

But a Fork is not a separate organisation. It's part of bits&bytes™ Foundation. That means:

- You operate under the Foundation's brand, safeguarding standards, and financial controls
- You don't have independent legal status
- You can't sign agreements or handle money on the Foundation's behalf without explicit written authority

## Step 2 — Read these pages first

Before you do anything else, make sure you've read:

- 🌐 Network Structure & Forks — what a Fork is and how recognition works
- 📜 Charter & Core Principles — the non-negotiables
- 🛡️ Safeguarding & Participant Safety — required reading for anyone running events
- 💳 The Financial Rules — money handling requirements
- 💰 Sponsorships & Financial Limits — before you approach any sponsor

## Step 3 — Know your authority limits

As a Fork Lead, you have real operational authority. You don't have legal or financial authority beyond what's been explicitly delegated to you in writing.

The things you CANNOT do without explicit written authorisation from the Foundation:

- Sign any agreement, MOU, or contract
- Accept, collect, or hold money for bits&bytes™ or your Fork
- Open any account or payment handle in the Fork's name
- Promise deliverables, exclusivity, or official partnerships to external parties
- Make any public statement that implies the Foundation has made a legal commitment

## Step 4 — Set up your Fork properly

- Confirm your Fork's recognition status and any conditions attached to it
- Identify your Local Maintainers and their specific responsibilities
- Set up communication channels (Discord, WhatsApp, etc.) following Foundation moderation standards
- Understand how to escalate concerns — who to contact and how
- Know what reporting the Foundation requires from your Fork

## Step 5 — Know when to escalate

Stop and escalate upstream whenever:

- A third party asks you for a signature, invoice, receipt, or formal commitment
- Money is being offered, requested, collected, reimbursed, or held
- A minor's safety, consent, wellbeing, or data may be affected
- Harassment, discrimination, bullying, or misconduct is alleged or observed
- A public statement might imply legal authority or institutional commitment
- You're uncertain whether you have authority to do something

Good-faith escalation is protected. You will never be penalised for slowing something down to check if it's safe to proceed.

## Step 6 — Records and handover

Your Fork's records, access credentials, event information, participant records, sponsorship communications, budgets, and digital assets don't belong to you personally. They're Foundation assets. If you step down from your role, everything gets handed over cleanly.

Fork Lead is a stewardship role. Not ownership of the Fork or its identity.
