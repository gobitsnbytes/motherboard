# 🌐 Network Structure & Forks

Owner: Bits Bytes

## What is bits&bytes™ Foundation?

bits&bytes™ Foundation is a youth-led, builder-first network for technology, education, and public-benefit work.

The Foundation exists to protect mission continuity, stewardship, safeguarding, infrastructure, and long-term coordination across the network.

---

## Mission

The Foundation exists to enable young builders, contributors, organisers, mentors, and communities to learn, build, collaborate, and contribute through locally adaptive technology, education, open-source, community, and public-benefit initiatives.

The Network is designed to be high-agency and locally creative, while remaining aligned to the Foundation's mission, standards, and protective boundaries.

## Structural model

- The Foundation acts as the upstream steward of the network
- Forks operate locally with real creative and operational autonomy
- Governance, safeguarding, infrastructure, and financial stewardship stay upstream
- Authority flows through written delegation, not titles or assumptions

---

## The Network model

The Foundation stewards a distributed network of locally autonomous Forks — the official operational units through which the Foundation recognises city-level, locality-level, institutional, digital, or thematic activity.

This is not a franchise system or branch-office network. Stewardship, governance, infrastructure, and brand continuity remain upstream with the Foundation.

A Fork is an official, local instantiation of the bits&bytes™ mission. It is not a branch office, not a licensed chapter, and not an independent club.

Forks are highly autonomous when it comes to visual identity, event formats, and local voice. The goal is not to clone the Foundation, but to build a native community that fits your city.

## The floor: what must not change

- The Code of Conduct must be preserved exactly as written
- The Founders section cannot be altered
- Use only the terminology "fork" or "bits&bytes™ [City]". Never use "franchise", "chapter", or "affiliate"
- You must remain teen-led and ship projects publicly

# How the network works

bits&bytes™ is built as a distributed network. The Foundation operates upstream — handling governance, finances, brand stewardship, safeguarding, infrastructure, and continuity. Forks are where all the local action happens: events, cohorts, communities, projects, hack nights.

The Foundation is the backbone, and Forks are the arms. The arms move freely and do their own thing locally, but they're connected to the same body, the same spine.

## What is a Fork?

A Fork is an official operational unit of the bits&bytes™ network. It could be city-level, locality-level, institutional (like a college chapter), digital, or thematic (like an AI-focused Fork or a hardware Fork).

Forks are officially recognised by the Foundation's Board. That recognition gives a Fork the right to:

- Use the bits&bytes™ name and brand for local activities
- Run events, cohorts, build nights, workshops
- Build a local builder community
- Mobilise local resources (under Foundation-approved frameworks)
- Adapt local culture, event formats, and programming to your context

## What a Fork is not

This is important:

- A Fork is not a separate legal entity. It can't register itself, sign contracts, or open bank accounts on its own.
- A Fork is not a franchise. There's no franchising deal — it's one Foundation, one brand.
- A Fork does not own the bits&bytes™ brand locally. Brand use is a permission, not ownership.
- Fork Leads do not have authority to sign agreements, accept legal obligations, or make financial commitments for the Foundation.
- A Fork cannot create a breakaway entity, open a parallel community using the bits&bytes™ name, or claim independent ownership of the Fork identity.

## Who leads a Fork?

Every Fork has a Fork Lead — someone recognised by the Foundation to provide local leadership, coordination, and accountability. Fork Leads can be teens. They usually are.

Being a Fork Lead means you're a steward of local execution and community coordination. It's a real leadership role with real responsibility — but it doesn't make you a legal representative of the Foundation. Your authority is operational, not legal.

A Fork may also have Local Maintainers — people who help run specific functions like tech, events, comms, or safeguarding within a Fork.

## How Forks get recognised (and lose recognition)

The Foundation's Board grants Fork Recognition. Recognition can be:

- Time-bound, conditional, or probationary
- Suspended if something goes wrong
- Archived if a Fork goes inactive
- Revived if an archived Fork gets new leadership and reapplies
- Revoked if there are serious violations (safeguarding, financial, brand misuse, mission drift)

If a Fork is suspended, archived, or revoked — all brand usage stops immediately. All assets, records, accounts, and credentials revert to the Foundation. No exceptions.

## The upstream-downstream model

Upstream is the Foundation. Downstream is Forks.

Upstream handles: mission continuity, brand protection, safeguarding standards, legal compliance, financial oversight, digital systems, and governance.

Forks handle: local events, community building, programming, culture, contributor recruitment, hack nights, local partnerships — all within the upstream boundaries.

Local autonomy is real and encouraged. But it's operational and creative autonomy — not legal or financial independence.
