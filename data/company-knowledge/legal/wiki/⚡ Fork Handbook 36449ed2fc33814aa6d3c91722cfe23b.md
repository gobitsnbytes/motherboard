# ⚡ Fork Handbook 36449ed2fc33814aa6d3c91722cfe23b

# ⚡ Fork Handbook

Owner: Bits Bytes
Tags: Onboarding

If a Fork is suspended, archived, or revoked:

1. Stop using the bits&bytes™ name, brand, domains, logos, and social accounts right away
2. Hand over records, assets, funds, credentials, and data linked to the Fork or the Foundation
3. Stop presenting the Fork as affiliated with bits&bytes™
4. Do not claim continuity, succession, or local ownership of the Fork identity

A Fork can be revived later, but these rules apply as soon as suspension starts.

## Escalate early

When in doubt, escalate. Good-faith escalation is protected. Nobody should be punished for pausing to check whether something is safe to do.

Escalation is how bits&bytes™ stays safe and lets young builders move quickly without taking on avoidable risk.

⚡

This is the main operating guide for Fork Leads and Local Maintainers. If local practice clashes with this page, this page wins.

</aside>

# Part 1 — What a Fork actually is

A Fork is the local operating unit of the bits&bytes™ network. It can be city-level, locality-level, institutional, digital, or thematic.

A Fork is not a separate organisation, not a franchise, not a branch office, and not an independent legal entity.

When a Fork is recognised, it gets the right to use the brand locally, run events, build community, and adapt to local context. That freedom is real, but it sits inside upstream guardrails.

<aside>
📌

Fork Recognition gives operational permission. It does not give authority to bind the Foundation or make financial commitments for it.

</aside>

## What your Fork can do

- Build local culture and choose the vibe that fits your city
- Run events, build nights, cohorts, workshops, and meetups
- Recruit contributors and maintainers
- Talk to local supporters and sponsors, but not commit the Foundation to anything
- Use Foundation-approved digital spaces
- Keep local records when asked
- Adapt to the local student and builder context

## What your Fork cannot do

- Register as its own society, club, company, or similar structure under the bits&bytes™ name
- Open bank accounts, UPI handles, wallets, or payment links as a Fork account
- Sign contracts, MOUs, sponsorships, venue agreements, or other binding documents
- Collect money through personal UPI, cash, or informal payment handles
- Claim ownership of the brand, domain, or Fork identity
- Create a breakaway group or parallel identity using bits&bytes™ goodwill
- Present itself as legally independent or separately authorised
- Create, register, or operate any external account, social media handle, page, email alias, payment link, bank account, or wallet in the Fork's or Foundation's name (or any confusingly similar name) unless it is centrally created and controlled by the Foundation, with all credentials remaining Foundation property

# Part 2 — Your role as Fork Lead

A Fork Lead is someone recognised to keep a Fork moving. The role is about local leadership, coordination, and accountability.

<aside>
🔐

The title is operational only. It does not create legal, financial, contracting, governance, or employment authority. It only exists where the Foundation has written it down.

</aside>

## What the title means

Being Fork Lead means you are trusted to run programming, coordinate builders, and keep the local team aligned. It does not mean you can commit the Foundation to anything.

No bio, email signature, event listing, social post, or past habit creates authority on its own.

## Youth leadership protections

bits&bytes™ is built on youth leadership. Minors and young participants may be Fork Leads, Local Maintainers, organisers, contributors, or community members.

That does not make you personally responsible for adult legal obligations. You are not personally liable just because you founded a project, led a Fork, spoke publicly, coordinated volunteers, hosted an event, or talked to a school or sponsor.

The limits exist to protect you, not slow you down.

## Managing your local team

Fork Leads may work with Local Maintainers who help with events, communications, technology, or safeguarding. Be clear about who owns what.

Nobody on the team has authority beyond what the Foundation has written down. Enthusiasm, reputation, or past contribution do not create authority.

## Records you must keep

Fork Leads and Local Maintainers must keep:

- Team contact details
- Activity plans and event notes
- Participant safety records where relevant
- Financial records for approved activity
- Incident records
- Sponsor and partner messages
- Any reports the Foundation asks for

These records belong to the Foundation, not to you personally, and they must be handed over when your role ends.

# Part 3 — Financial rules

Every rupee, sponsorship, donation, event fee, reimbursement, or other economic benefit connected with bits&bytes™ or your Fork must go through Foundation-approved financial systems. This is the central financial rule.

<aside>
🚨

Do not use personal UPI, personal bank routing, cash collections, local or informal accounts, personal wallets, unapproved payment links, or any account or QR code presented as a Foundation or Fork account.

</aside>

## How money works at a Fork

A Fork does not have its own financial life. All money connected to a Fork stays under Foundation control.

If an event needs a budget, spend, or reimbursement, check with the Foundation first and get it approved in writing.

## Sponsorships and local support

You can talk to local companies, colleges, and community groups for support. You can say you are part of bits&bytes™.

What you cannot do is commit the Foundation to anything, promise deliverables, or collect money from them.

If interest turns real, escalate upstream. The Foundation handles the paperwork and the routing.

## What happens to what your Fork creates

Anything created for a Fork — including posters, code, websites, event content, design assets, curricula, and documentation — is stewarded by the Foundation.

You get credit. You do not license it out, take it with you if the Fork closes, or sub-license it on your own.

# Part 4 — Running events

Events are the heartbeat of a Fork. You have room to shape the format, theme, vibe, and length.

Every event still needs:

- A safe venue
- Clear attendance control
- Consent checks when minors are involved
- An incident reporting path before the event starts
- Budget approval before any spend, promise, or vendor commitment
- Zero tolerance for harassment, bullying, grooming, discrimination, or exclusion

The Foundation may also require extra safeguards for specific events, especially when minors or vulnerable participants are involved.

# Part 5 — When to stop and escalate

Escalation is not failure. Escalation is the mechanism that keeps bits&bytes™ safe, legal, and a place where young builders can do real things without getting personally hurt. If in doubt, stop and ask before acting. (IOM §8.1)

<aside>
🚦

You must pause and escalate immediately if any of the following arise: a third party asks for a signature, invoice, receipt, commitment, or formal approval • money is being offered, requested, collected, reimbursed, or held • a minor's safety, consent, wellbeing, or data may be affected • harassment, discrimination, bullying, grooming, coercion, or retaliation is alleged • a digital account, repository, or admin right may be misused • a public statement may imply legal authority or institutional commitment • a sponsor, school, partner, donor, or vendor asks for deliverables or exclusivity • there is any uncertainty about whether written authority exists for an action. (IOM §8.2)

</aside>

Good-faith escalation is always protected. You will never be penalised for pausing an event, refusing to collect money, declining to sign something, reporting misconduct, or asking for upstream review. Retaliation against someone who escalates a concern in good faith is itself a breach. (IOM §8.3 & §4.9)

# Part 6 — Safeguarding

Safeguarding is a core operating priority. It is not a compliance checkbox. Every Fork, event, cohort, digital community, group chat, workshop, school partnership, mentorship arrangement, and local initiative must comply with the Foundation's safeguarding and participant wellbeing standards. (IOM §4.1–4.2)

- Where minors are involved, follow all consent, supervision, communication, venue, travel, recording, photography, data, and platform-access requirements — no convenience exceptions
- Harassment, bullying, intimidation, stalking, grooming, coercion, retaliation, sexual misconduct, discriminatory conduct, and hostile behaviour are prohibited in all Foundation-connected settings — offline and online
- Digital communities you run must be moderated — access controls, content removal, warnings, and escalation paths all apply
- Every Fork must have a clear and communicated path for reporting safety, misconduct, financial irregularity, and data misuse — and reports must be escalated promptly
- Incident records must be handled with care, confidentiality, and appropriate access limits

# Part 7 — When your Fork is suspended, archived, or revoked

The Foundation may suspend, archive, or revoke Fork Recognition for non-compliance, inactivity, safeguarding concerns, misuse of brand or IP, financial irregularity, legal risk, mission drift, reputational risk, or any other reason considered sufficient by the Board. This is a protective power, not a punitive one. (IOM §7.5 | Network Governance Charter §2.12)

When suspension, archival, or revocation takes effect:

1. All use of the bits&bytes™ name, brand, domains, logos, social accounts, and digital spaces stops immediately
2. All records, assets, funds, credentials, data, and materials relating to the Fork or the Foundation must be handed over promptly
3. You stop representing yourself or your group as affiliated with, recognised by, authorised by, or connected to bits&bytes™
4. No claim to continuity, succession, ownership, or any rights in the Fork identity or Foundation goodwill
5. No creation, incorporation, or operation of any parallel entity, confusingly similar name, brand, community, or account

Fork Revival — being restored to recognised status — is possible. But the above obligations apply from the moment of suspension onwards, regardless. (Network Governance Charter §2.11–2.14)

# Part 8 — Breaches and corrective action

Breach of this handbook includes: unsafe conduct, unauthorised financial activity, unauthorised commitments, misuse of brand, failure to escalate safeguarding concerns, retaliation, misuse of digital access, misleading claims of authority, refusal to follow approved controls, or any conduct that creates avoidable legal, financial, safety, or reputational risk to the Foundation or its participants. (IOM §9.1)

The Foundation may respond with: directions, required training, suspension of an event, removal from a role, revocation of digital access, project freeze, Fork suspension or archive, revocation of brand permission, or requirement to repay or document funds. Failure to enforce a rule in one situation does not waive the Foundation's right to enforce it later. (IOM §9.2–9.3)
