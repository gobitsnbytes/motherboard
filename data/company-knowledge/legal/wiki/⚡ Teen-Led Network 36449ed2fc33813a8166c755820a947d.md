# ⚡ Teen-Led Network

Owner: Bits Bytes

## Youth leadership is foundational

Youth leadership is not a feature of bits&bytes™ — it is the model. The Foundation is explicitly designed to be youth-led in its programmes, operations, and community.

Minors and young participants may act as:

- Contributors, organisers, Fork Leads, Local Maintainers
- Executive Officers (CEO, CTO, COO, etc.)
- Volunteers, participants, or community members

… to the extent **lawful and subject to Board-approved safeguards**.

---

## Protected operational participation

Participation by a minor in the Network, a Fork, an event, an online community, or an executive, operational, community, or leadership capacity is **protected operational participation**.

This means such participation does **not**, by itself:

- Impose adult legal responsibility on the minor
- Create fiduciary authority or personal liability
- Give authority to bind the Foundation
- Create unrestricted legal authority

---

## What minors cannot do independently

No minor may independently:

- Bind the Foundation or sign legally binding instruments
- Negotiate contracts, MoUs, or sponsorship terms
- Independently handle Protected Funds or operate financial channels
- Accept legal obligations or give guarantees
- Make representations of legal authority
- Approve sponsorship terms, issue invoices, or open accounts

… unless the action is **lawful, properly supervised, and expressly authorised in writing** by the Board within the Authority Matrix or a specific written delegation.

---

## Titles are operational, not legal

Titles such as Fork Lead, Local Maintainer, organiser, contributor, administrator, coordinator, executive, ambassador, mentor, or volunteer describe **operational responsibility only**.

They do not create:

- Legal authority or financial authority
- Employment status, partnership status, or agency status
- Personal ownership of any Foundation activity

---

## Adult oversight where required

The Board may require:

- Adult supervision for activities involving minors
- Parent or guardian consent
- Event risk assessment and safe venue requirements
- Digital access limits and two-adult rules
- Escalation contacts and mentor oversight

---

## Why these protections exist

These limits exist **not to slow down young builders** — but to let young builders move fast without accidentally:

- Becoming personally liable
- Putting funds at risk
- Making unauthorised commitments
- Exposing themselves, the Foundation, or the community to avoidable harm

> 🏗️ The goal is maximum creative freedom within a legal and financial safety net.
>

## IP assignment and minors

Under Indian law (Indian Contract Act, 1872), a minor cannot enter a valid contract. Any IP assignment by a minor is void.

For minor contributors, IP assignment should be:

- Obtained through a parent or guardian on the minor's behalf, or
- Deferred until the contributor reaches majority

This should be handled via a Board-approved contributor agreement template. See the **Intellectual Property & Digital Systems** page.

The bits&bytes™ ecosystem is entirely teen-led. Fork Leads are the primary drivers of community activity. You are expected to operate asynchronously by default, utilizing GitHub and Discord.

If a fork loses its teen leadership, it loses its Fork status. We do not allow adults to run Forks.

## The Leads Council

The Leads Council is an advisory body where Fork Leads can collaborate, discuss cross-city initiatives, and surface issues. However, it is not a legal decision-making board and cannot override Foundation policies.

# The Teen-Led Network

bits&bytes™ is genuinely youth-led. That's not a marketing line — it's built into how we're set up. Teens and young people can be Fork Leads, Local Maintainers, Executive Officers, cohort leads, and community builders. Minors can hold real operational roles.

But being youth-led comes with a thoughtful structure. We've designed it so young builders can move fast and own things — while being protected from adult legal responsibilities they shouldn't carry.

## What youth participation actually means

Participating in bits&bytes™ — whether you're a Fork Lead, a hackathon organiser, a cohort member, or just a community contributor — is protected operational participation.

That means your participation does NOT automatically make you personally responsible for:

- Legal obligations of the Foundation
- Financial commitments made by Forks or events
- Adult-level liability just because you held a title or ran a project
- Contracts or sponsorship deals — even if you were the one talking to a sponsor

## Titles are operational, not legal

"Fork Lead," "Local Maintainer," "CTO," "Ambassador" — these are real titles with real operational responsibility. But they describe what you do, not your legal authority.

No title at bits&bytes™ gives you the right to sign contracts, open bank accounts, collect money, or make commitments on behalf of the Foundation unless you've been explicitly authorised to do that in writing.

## What minors cannot do independently

No matter your title or role, if you're a minor, you cannot independently:

- Sign or negotiate legally binding documents
- Handle, collect, or hold Foundation or Fork funds
- Open accounts or financial instruments
- Accept legal obligations on behalf of the Foundation
- Issue invoices or make financial representations

This is not because we don't trust you. It's because we do — and we want to make sure you're never accidentally put in a position of adult legal responsibility you didn't sign up for.

## The builder-friendly principle

All these rules exist for one reason: to let young builders move fast without accidentally becoming personally liable, putting funds at risk, making unauthorised commitments, or exposing the Foundation and its community to avoidable harm.

The guardrails are there so you can run. Not so you get slowed down.

## Adult oversight

For events or activities involving minors, the Foundation may require adult supervision, parent/guardian consent, safe venue checks, and escalation contacts. This is standard — it protects everyone.

If you're a Fork Lead running an event with minors in the audience or on the team: check what's required, and escalate early if you're unsure.

## What every youth-led Fork must do

- Stay within the Foundation's mission, brand rules, safeguarding standards, and financial controls
- Avoid unsafe venues, unsafe online practices, harassment, discrimination, bullying, or exclusionary conduct
- Use only approved channels for money, sponsorships, institutional communications, and public commitments
- Escalate safety concerns, financial questions, and authority questions early
- Accept suspension or modification if the Board identifies a risk
