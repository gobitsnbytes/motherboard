## The document hierarchy

The Foundation operates under a simple internal governance stack. If there is any conflict, the order of precedence is:

1. Foundation Charter — the core statement of purpose, structure, and operating principles
2. Internal Operating Manual — the day-to-day rulebook for how the Network works
3. Authority Matrix — the role-by-role decision map for spending, sign-off, access, and approvals
4. Fork Protocols and Network Policies — detailed operating rules for local execution
5. Board resolutions and written delegations — specific decisions and limits issued from time to time

Any informal practice, local habit, chat message, badge, event listing, or social post that conflicts with the above is overridden.

Welcome to the official Fork Charter. This document defines what a Fork is, the autonomy you have, and the hard limits that keep the network safe and aligned.

## What is a Fork?

A Fork is an official, local instantiation of the bits&bytes™ mission — getting teens to build and ship publicly. It is not a franchise, a licensed chapter, a club, or a separate legal entity.

You have the freedom to adapt events, community culture, and local projects to your city's energy. But because you operate under the bits&bytes™ Foundation, you must follow strict non-negotiables: you cannot sign legal contracts, handle sponsorships via personal bank accounts, or make binding promises on behalf of the Foundation.

## Core principles

- Teen-led: Fork leads must be students. This is a non-negotiable part of our DNA.
- Public shipping: All projects built at the Fork must ship publicly. We are a network of builders.
- Safeguarding first: The safety of the community overrides local autonomy. Any serious misconduct must be escalated immediately.
- Upstream authority: The bits&bytes™ Foundation (the "Upstream" Board) retains all legal, financial, and final dispute-resolution authority.

Review the rest of the wiki for guidelines on how to run your Fork securely and effectively.

# Our charter & core principles

bits&bytes™ is built on a clear set of principles that govern how the Foundation, Forks, Fork Leads, and every participant operates. These are operational commitments baked into how we're structured.

## 1. We are one Foundation

There is one bits&bytes™ Foundation. Forks are not separate organizations. They don't have their own legal identity. Every Fork operates as part of the same Foundation, under the same mission, the same safeguarding standards, and the same brand.

The bits&bytes™ name is an unregistered trademark (™) of GOBITSNBYTES FOUNDATION.

The Foundation is the upstream constitutional steward of the entire network. The Board holds constitutional, fiduciary, and governance authority. That authority doesn't get delegated away just because a Fork is locally autonomous.

## 2. Local autonomy is real — and it has limits

Every Fork can adapt locally. Your city's Fork can have its own culture, its own programming style, its own community personality. That's by design. We want local ownership.

But local autonomy is operational and creative. It is not:

- Legal independence
- Authority to bind the Foundation
- Ownership of the brand
- A right to vary safeguarding or compliance requirements

You can't override upstream requirements locally. If there's ever a conflict between local practice and the Foundation's standards — the Foundation's standards win. Always.

## 3. Authority only flows through written delegation

This is probably the most important principle in the whole system.

No one at bits&bytes™ has authority to do something just because of their title, their role, or because they've done it before. Authority flows through written delegation from the Board — that's it.

Being a Fork Lead doesn't automatically give you authority to sign contracts. Being an exec doesn't automatically give you authority to spend money. No title, badge, email, or public role creates implied or apparent authority to bind the Foundation.

If you're unsure whether you have authority to do something — escalate. That's the right call every time.

## 4. Safeguarding is non-negotiable

Because we work with young people — many of them minors — safeguarding is a core operating priority, not an afterthought. Every Fork, every event, every digital community, every cohort must meet the Foundation's safeguarding standards.

No Fork can lower the safeguarding bar for convenience. No Fork can opt out of incident reporting. No one can retaliate against someone who raises a safety concern.

## 5. Money stays in the system

All money connected to bits&bytes™ — sponsorships, donations, event fees, grants — must flow through Foundation-approved financial channels. Period.

No personal UPI collections. No informal bank accounts. No cash handling outside approved structures. No one personally holds Foundation funds.

This isn't bureaucracy. It's what keeps young builders protected from financial liability.

## 6. What we're building towards

The Foundation exists to preserve and grow a youth-led, builder-first, technology education network. We explicitly do not want to drift into:

- Private profit or personal enrichment
- Political partisanship
- Franchise monetisation
- Uncontrolled affiliate structures
- Activities inconsistent with our not-for-profit character

Mission continuity is a governance priority. The Board is responsible for making sure we don't drift.
