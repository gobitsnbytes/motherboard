# 🎨 Brand & Intellectual Property

Owner: Bits Bytes
Tags: Design

## Ownership

All intellectual property created, developed, authored, designed, configured, contributed, adapted, or generated by employees, Executive Officers, Fork Leads, Local Maintainers, volunteers, contributors, interns, or participants in the course of or in connection with the Foundation's activities vests in the Foundation — to the fullest extent permitted by law and subject to any separate written agreement approved by the Board.

---

## Scope of protected assets

Intellectual property and network assets include:

- Copyrights, trademarks, service marks, trade names, logos
- Domain names, websites, documentation, curricula, lesson plans
- Software, code, repositories, databases, datasets
- Videos, photographs, designs, graphics, media
- Event formats, programme frameworks, playbooks, policies, templates
- Brand systems, digital credentials, communications channels
- Goodwill associated with the bits&bytes™ name and Network

---

## Assignment documentation

The Board may require employees, Executive Officers, consultants, volunteers, Fork Leads, Local Maintainers, mentors, contributors, interns, partners, or participants to execute:

- IP assignment documentation
- Contributor licence agreements
- Confidentiality agreements
- Data protection documentation
- Moral rights waivers

Note for minors: under Indian contract law, a minor cannot enter a valid contract. IP assignment by a minor is void. For minor contributors, assignment must be obtained through a parent or guardian, or deferred until the contributor reaches majority, via a Board-approved contributor agreement template.

---

## Open-source and open-content

The Board may authorise release of software, curricula, documentation, educational resources, or datasets under:

- Open-source licences
- Open-content or Creative Commons licences
- Similar publicly available licences

…where consistent with the Foundation's objects, safeguarding obligations, third-party rights, and legal compliance.

No Fork or participant may independently licence Foundation-owned or Foundation-stewarded assets except under written authority or applicable published licensing terms approved by the Board.

---

## Attribution and recognition

The Foundation may recognise contributors through attribution, credits, commit history, acknowledgements, or public showcases.

Attribution does not:

- Alter upstream stewardship or ownership
- Create licensing or brand control entitlements
- Give any right to continue using the brand after recognition ends

---

## Digital systems authority

The Board has authority over:

- Websites, repositories, and domains
- Social media accounts and communication platforms
- Databases, mailing lists, and design systems
- Cloud services, analytics, automation tools, and access controls

Administration rights may be granted to Executive Officers, Fork Leads, Local Maintainers, volunteers, or participants only under written limits and access controls approved by or under authority of the Board.

Such access:

- Can be monitored, transferred, restricted, or revoked at any time
- Is a delegated tool of stewardship, not a personal right
- Does not equal ownership, authority, or any entitlement to continued access

---

## Third-party rights

The Foundation and all Forks, employees, volunteers, and contributors must:

- Respect third-party intellectual property rights
- Avoid infringement, plagiarism, unauthorised copying, or licence violations
- Not import, publish, copy, train on, distribute, or use third-party materials in a way that creates legal, reputational, security, or ethical risk

---

## Unauthorised use consequences

Unauthorised use of the Foundation's IP, brand, digital systems, data, or confidential information may result in:

- Suspension or removal from role
- Fork Archive or Fork Revocation
- Withdrawal of brand permission
- Recovery of assets and takedown requests
- Civil or criminal action under applicable law

Anything built under the bits&bytes™ banner — code, design, content, curricula, docs — belongs to the Foundation. Forks included. Contributors included.

You will always be credited for what you build, but you cannot take the Fork's identity or assets with you if you leave or if the Fork closes.

You cannot use third-party IP without permission, and you cannot license Foundation IP to others.

# Brand & intellectual property

## Who owns the brand?

The Foundation is the steward and owner of the bits&bytes™ name, logos, marks, domains, design systems, repositories, websites, curricula, documentation, educational content, digital systems, and everything else associated with the network.

This includes everything built by anyone — employees, Fork Leads, Local Maintainers, volunteers, contributors, participants — in the course of or connected with Foundation activities. Intellectual property vests in the Foundation to the fullest extent permitted by law.

## What's covered

- The name "bits&bytes™" and all variations (including "bitsnbytes" where the ampersand character is unavailable, unsupported, or restricted)
- Logos, marks, trade names, design systems
- Domain names, social media accounts, email systems
- Repositories, source code, platforms, databases
- Curricula, lesson plans, event formats, playbooks
- Educational videos, content, written materials
- Policies, templates, governance documents
- Community goodwill and bits&bytes™ brand identity

## How Forks can use the brand

Recognised Forks, partners, schools, volunteers, and contributors may use the Foundation's name, logos, marks, domains, and brand elements — but only in accordance with brand permissions, Fork Protocols, and written approvals from the Foundation.

This use is:

- Limited — only for approved purposes
- Conditional — tied to continued recognition and compliance
- Revocable — can be withdrawn if there's misuse, suspension, or revocation
- Not ownership — using the brand doesn't mean you own it locally

## What you cannot do with the brand

- Create a separate entity using the bits&bytes™ name (or anything confusingly similar)
- Use the brand after your Fork's recognition is suspended, archived, or revoked
- Claim brand ownership, independence, or succession rights
- Sub-license or re-grant brand rights to other groups without Board approval
- Present any Fork or unofficial group as the legal continuation of bits&bytes™

## Open-source & open-content

The Foundation may authorise release of software, curricula, documentation, educational resources, and datasets under open-source or Creative Commons licences where that's consistent with our mission.

No Fork or participant can independently license Foundation-owned assets. Open-source licensing decisions go through the Board.

## Digital systems authority

The Board controls all Foundation digital systems — websites, repos, domains, social accounts, comms platforms, cloud services, analytics, access controls.

If you're given admin access to a Foundation digital asset, that access is a delegated tool of stewardship, not a personal right. It can be monitored, transferred, restricted, or revoked at any time for safety, continuity, or governance reasons.

## Visual elements

The brand uses a library of textural and graphic shapes to add energy and motion:

- Gradient blobs: soft, glowing orange-to-burgundy radial gradients; convey warmth and dynamism
- Halftone dots: circular dot-pattern shapes; add retro print texture
- Burst / starburst shapes: bold, spiky forms in burgundy; signal attention
- Star shapes: irregular multi-pointed stars in black and burgundy; used as editorial accents

These elements should feel spontaneous but not chaotic — pair them with generous white space and the structured typography system.

## The name

There are two names. Use them correctly.

**bits&bytes™** is the public brand name. Use this in all community-facing content, events, social, and design work. Always lowercase. The ampersand is part of the name.

**GOBITSNBYTES FOUNDATION** is the registered legal entity name. Use this only in legal documents, contracts, invoices, regulatory filings, and official correspondence. All caps. Never in community-facing creative work.

<aside>
❌ Never write: "bits & bytes™", "Bits & Bytes", "bits and bytes", "B&B", "gobitsnbytes", or any other variation in public-facing creative work. The name is bits&bytes™ — exactly that.

## The trademark symbol

bits&bytes™ is an unregistered trademark (™) of GOBITSNBYTES FOUNDATION. The ™ symbol may be used in formal contexts — brand decks, official website headers, legal-adjacent materials. It is optional in casual community contexts like Discord, social captions, and event flyers. Never use ® — the mark is not yet registered.

The logo is Foundation-owned IP. All versions — wordmark, icon, lockups — are controlled assets.

## What you can and can't put the brand on

You have brand permission for your Fork or role. Here's how that works in practice.

### Allowed without further approval

- Event graphics for bits&bytes™-sanctioned events (following visual identity guidelines)
- Social posts about your Fork's activities, using the approved name and assets
- Community content (Discord banners, recap posts, photos from events)
- Educational materials produced for bits&bytes™ programmes

### Requires approval from CCO / Head of Brand & Media

- Merchandise (physical or digital) with the bits&bytes™ brand
- Co-branded materials with a sponsor, partner, school, or external organisation
- Press kits, media outreach, or anything going to journalists or publications
- Any asset that implies the Foundation is a sponsor of, partner with, or endorses an external entity

### Never allowed

- Using the brand to raise funds, collect money, or solicit sponsorships without Board authorisation
- Creating sub-brands, alternate logos, or visual systems under the bits&bytes™ name
- Using the brand after your Fork is archived, suspended, or your role has ended
- Producing anything that implies the Foundation, its Directors, or its Executive Officers personally endorse a product, person, service, or political position
- Any content that violates the Code of Conduct

## Legal notices on creative assets

Every public-facing creative asset carrying the bits&bytes™ name should include the following. Small text at the bottom of the asset is fine — it doesn't need to compete with the design.

### Must include

- **`© 2026 GOBITSNBYTES FOUNDATION`** — asserts copyright ownership under the Copyright Act, 1957. The year should reflect the year of publication. Use this on every asset.
- **™ symbol on bits&bytes™** — bits&bytes™ is an unregistered trademark of GOBITSNBYTES FOUNDATION. The ™ symbol should appear at least once on the asset (on the wordmark or in the footer). It puts the public on notice of the claim.

### Good practice to include

- **All rights reserved.** — Technically implied under Indian law, but standard globally. Signals that the asset cannot be reproduced or redistributed without permission.
- **gobitsnbytes.org** — The canonical website. Ties the creative back to the Foundation officially.

### Recommended footer format

<aside>
🗒️ **bits&bytes™** by GOBITSNBYTES FOUNDATION
© 2026 GOBITSNBYTES FOUNDATION. All rights reserved. | gobitsnbytes.org

</aside>

### What you must never use

- **®** — The registered trademark symbol. bits&bytes™ is not yet a registered mark. Using ® is a misrepresentation and may constitute an offence under the Trade Marks Act, 1999.
- Patent or trade secret notices — not applicable to community creative assets
- Long legal disclaimers in the body of the creative — not required and not appropriate for recruitment or community-facing work

# Pre-publish checklist

<aside>
✅ Run through this before publishing anything with the bits&bytes™ name on it. Every point. Every time.

</aside>

---

## Name & trademark

- Is the name written as **`bits&bytes™`** — lowercase, with the ampersand? No caps, no variations
- Does the ™ symbol appear at least once on the asset?
- Is the footer copyright notice correct — year, entity name, website?

---

## What the creative claims

- Is the description of bits&bytes™ factually accurate? (Network / Foundation — not “club”, “startup”, “company”, “NGO”)
- Are any stats, rankings, or superlatives (“India’s largest”, “best”, “#1”) actually verifiable? If not, cut them
- Does anything imply a partnership, sponsorship, or endorsement that hasn’t been formally approved in writing?

---

## Third-party content

- Every image, illustration, font, icon, music track, or video clip has a licence that permits this specific use — commercial or non-profit, attribution requirements checked
- If AI-generated images are used — verify the tool’s terms explicitly permit use in published non-profit content
- Stock photos of real people — does the licence explicitly cover use in promotional or recruitment material?

---

## People’s likeness

- Any photo or video of a real person — do you have their consent for this specific use?
- If the person is a minor — do you have verified parental or guardian consent?
- No one’s face, name, or image is used in a way that implies they endorse something they haven’t explicitly agreed to

---

## Sensitive language

- Nothing on the asset could reasonably be read as discriminatory, exclusionary, or inappropriate — run it through the CoC filter
- If there’s hacker or builder culture slang — ask whether this asset will reach schools, parents, institutional partners, or press, not just the core community

---

## Money

- The creative does not ask people to pay, donate, or contribute funds unless that specific campaign has Board sign-off
- Sponsor acknowledgement on the creative has written approval from both the sponsor and the Foundation (CCO or Board depending on scale)

---

## Co-branding

- Another organisation’s logo appears on the asset → written approval from that organisation + CCO sign-off before publishing
- Partner or sponsor logos appear only in a size, position, and context they have approved

---

## Platform rules

- The asset complies with the content policies of the platform it’s being published on (Instagram, LinkedIn, YouTube, WhatsApp, etc.) — independently of Foundation rules
- Promotional content disclosures are included where required by platform policy (e.g. sponsored posts)
- Music used in video content is cleared for the specific platform — a track licenced for Instagram may not be cleared for YouTube

---

## Data in visuals

- No personal data is visible in the asset — check for email addresses, phone numbers, full names of private individuals, ID documents, or financial figures
- Event registrations, attendance sheets, or participant photos do not appear without explicit individual consent

---

## Access & off-boarding

- The account or tool this is published from is logged in the access register, not tied to personal credentials
- If the team member managing this asset leaves, there is a handover plan in place
