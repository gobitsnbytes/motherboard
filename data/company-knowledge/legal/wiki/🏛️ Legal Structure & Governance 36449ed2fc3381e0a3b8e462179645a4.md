## Members
Members are the guarantors of the Foundation — the persons whose names appear in the register of members.
- Membership is controlled and limited. Admission requires a written application and approval by at least three-fourths of Directors.
- The Foundation has no share capital and no equity shares. Members guarantee a nominal sum (up to Rs. 10,000) in the event of winding up. This is a governance instrument, not an investment.
- Each member has one vote at general meetings.
- Membership does not arise from participation, employment, contribution, Fork leadership, donation, or length of association.
- Members may also serve as Directors (Member-Directors) but may not receive remuneration for being members alone.
## Board of Directors
The Board holds ultimate legal, financial, safeguarding, and governance authority over the Foundation.
- Minimum: 2 Directors; Maximum: 10 Directors
- Directors need not be Members
- The Board meets at least once every six calendar months
- Quorum: 2 Directors
- Resolutions may be passed at meetings or by circular resolution
- The Board adopts and amends the Authority Matrix, Fork Protocols, Network Policies, and operational policies

## Executive Officers
Executive Officers are appointed by the Board for operational management — technology, programmes, finance, compliance, community, and so on. Common roles include CEO, CTO, COO, CFO, Company Secretary, and Network operations leads.
- Titles represent accountability domains, not rigid silos. Roles may overlap across functions.
- Authority is delegated, revocable, and bounded by the Authority Matrix, appointment terms, and Board supervision.
- Employment or a public-facing title does not confer membership, directorship, or ownership.
- Founders or youth leaders may be appointed as remunerated Executive Officers for actual services rendered.
- No public-facing title, email signature, or informal course of dealing creates implied or apparent authority.
---
## Separation of governance and operations
This is how authority actually flows:
- Members and Directors exercise constitutional, fiduciary, and governance authority.
- Executive Officers and employees exercise delegated operational authority only.
- Fork Leads, Local Maintainers, and contributors exercise community-level execution only.
Authority flows through written delegation, not assumption, title, seniority, or practical convenience.
---
## Conflict of interest
Any Director, Member, founder, or Executive Officer with a direct or indirect interest in any appointment, remuneration, related-party arrangement, procurement, IP assignment, sponsorship, grant, contract, or payment must:
1. Disclose the interest fully and promptly to the Board
2. Recuse from deliberation and abstain from voting
3. Provide factual information only if requested
The disclosure, abstention, and basis of approval must be recorded in minutes or a written resolution.

Section 8 means the Foundation is a not-for-profit. All income — from sponsorships, events, fees, grants, or any other source — must be applied solely to its charitable and educational mission. No one takes home profits. That's the law.
As a company limited by guarantee, the Foundation has no share capital and no equity shares. Members guarantee a nominal sum (up to Rs. 10,000) in the event of winding up — a governance instrument consistent with the not-for-profit structure, as set out in the Memorandum of Association.
## The Board of Directors
The Board is the highest governance authority. Directors are appointed through the correct legal process and collectively manage the Foundation's affairs.
The Board is responsible for:
- Approving strategy, budgets, and policies
- Recognising, suspending, and revoking Forks
- Setting and enforcing the Authority Matrix
- Protecting the Foundation's brand, IP, and finances
- Maintaining safeguarding and compliance standards
- Opening and controlling all financial accounts
## Executive Officers
The Board can appoint Executive Officers — CEO, CTO, COO, and others — for day-to-day operational management. These are real leadership roles with real accountability.
- Their authority comes from written delegation by the Board, not from their title alone.
- They can be young people and minors (this is a youth-led org).
- Being an Executive Officer does not make you a Director or legal Member of the Foundation.
- Their authority can be expanded, restricted, or revoked by the Board at any time.
## Membership (the governance kind)
"Members" in the legal sense are guarantor members of the Foundation — a small, mission-aligned governance group. This is not the same as being a community member, Fork participant, or contributor.
Membership is not conferred by:
- Being a Fork Lead
- Contributing to the org
- Donating or sponsoring
- Length of association with the Foundation
## The Authority Matrix
The Authority Matrix specifies exactly who can do what — spend money, sign agreements, hire people, approve events, manage platforms. If something isn't in the Authority Matrix, you don't have authority to do it. Ask first.
## What this means for you
If you're a Fork Lead, Local Maintainer, volunteer, or participant: you're protected by this structure. No one can accidentally make you personally liable for something the Foundation did. Your role is operational. The legal responsibility sits with the Foundation.
In return: you don't act on behalf of the Foundation unless you've been explicitly authorised in writing to do so.

## Governing documents
The Foundation's governance operates through a hierarchy of documents. Each layer is subordinate to the one above it.
- **e-MOA (e-Memorandum of Association)** — Defines the company's name, registered state, 18 objects (A–R), non-distribution clause, winding-up rules, and amalgamation restriction. Signed 1 June 2026. Filed with the Registrar of Companies, Uttar Pradesh.
- **e-AOA (e-Articles of Association)** — Defines Board composition, proceedings, voting, Executive Officer appointments (Article 28), Fork and Node definitions, and the full constitutional glossary. Table H (company limited by guarantee, no share capital). Signed 1 June 2026.
- **Network Governance Charter (NGC) v2.0** — Constitutional operating instrument for the bits&bytes™ Network. Establishes the Fork model, separation of powers, IP and brand ownership, and Section 8 financial constraints. Adopted by the Board on 4 June 2026.
- **Internal Operating Manual (IOM) v2.0** — Operational rulebook covering youth leadership protections, safeguarding, financial controls, the Authority Matrix, Fork operating rules, and escalation procedures. Adopted by the Board on 4 June 2026.
- **Code of Conduct v1.0** — Community standards document. Binding on all participants, Fork Leads, Executive Officers, volunteers, and guests across all bits&bytes™ spaces and events. Adopted 4 June 2026. Published at gobitsnbytes.org/coc.
> ⚠️ Only the e-MOA and e-AOA can be altered with prior Registrar of Companies approval (e-MOA Clause 6). The NGC, IOM, and CoC may be amended by Board Resolution.
